The Last Winter Letter

By Evelyn Hart

                                             Some Messages Are Meant to Arrive Late By Evelyn Hart

The letter arrived on the coldest morning of winter. Maya found it beneath her front door, covered in tiny drops of melted snow.There was no stamp.
No address.
Only her name.
She opened it.
Inside was a single sentence: “If you are reading this, I didn't make it home.”
Maya's heart stopped. The letter was signed with a name she hadn't seen in eight years. Elias.
Her childhood best friend had disappeared during a snowstorm when they were ten. No one had ever found him.
Maya read the letter again. At the bottom was a date. December 18, 2018. Tomorrow.

She rushed to the old train station where they had last been seen together. The platform was empty except for a wooden bench buried beneath snow.

Maya remembered their childhood game: whenever they wanted to leave a secret message, they hid it beneath that bench. She dug through the snow. Nothing.
Then she noticed a loose wooden plank. Behind it was a small metal box. Inside was another letter. Her hands trembled as she opened it.
“Maya, if you found this, I finally know what happened.”

The letter explained that Elias had survived the storm. He had been rescued by a passing train and taken to another town, but he had lost his memory and never remembered his own name.
Maya looked at the final line.
“Tomorrow, train 27 will stop here at 6:10.”

The next evening, Maya stood on the platform. Snow began to fall. At exactly 6:10, a train appeared through the mist.

The doors opened.
A young man stepped onto the platform. He looked around nervously. Then his eyes met Maya's.
He smiled.
“I don't know why,” he said, “but I feel like I've been looking for you.”

Maya smiled through her tears.For eight years, she had waited for an answer. She finally had one. And that winter, the last letter finally reached its destination.
