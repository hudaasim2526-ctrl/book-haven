The House That Whispered At Night

Isla Bennett

                                             Some doors are better left unopened. By Isla Bennett

Everyone in Willow Lane knew about the abandoned house at the end of the street. Its windows were always dark. Its garden had disappeared beneath tangled weeds, and its front door had been locked for as long as anyone could remember.

But there was one strange thing about the house.
Every night at exactly midnight, someone whispered from inside.
Maya had heard the story countless times. She never believed it.
Until one rainy night. She was sitting beside her bedroom window when the clock on her wall reached midnight. Then she heard it.
“Come closer…”
Maya stopped breathing. The whisper had come from the abandoned house. She looked outside.
The house stood silently beneath the rain. Then, without warning, its upstairs window lit up.
Maya saw a shadow standing behind the glass. She blinked.
It was gone.
The next evening, curiosity got the better of her.
Maya walked to the house with a flashlight. The front door was locked when she reached it—but before she could touch the handle, it slowly opened by itself.

Inside, the air was strangely cold. Dust covered everything except an old grandfather clock standing in the hallway.Its hands were frozen at 11:59.
Maya stepped closer.
Tick.
The clock moved.
Tick.
She backed away. Then it struck midnight. A whisper floated down the staircase. “Maya…”
She froze. Nobody knew she was there.
She followed the voice upstairs and found a narrow hallway ending at a small wooden door. The door opened before she touched it. Inside was a room filled with old photographs. Maya lifted her flashlight.
Every photograph showed the house. Different years.
Different families.
But the same hallway.
The same clock.

Then she saw the newest photograph. Her hands began to shake. It showed Maya standing inside the room. And the photograph had been taken only moments ago.
She dropped it.
A second whisper came from behind her. “Don't turn around.”
Maya stood completely still.
Her flashlight flickered.
Then she heard footsteps behind her.
One step.
Then another.
She ran.
She raced down the stairs and threw open the front door. The moment she stepped outside, the door slammed shut behind her. Maya didn't look back. She ran all the way home.

That night, she locked her bedroom door and tried to convince herself it had all been a nightmare.
Then her phone buzzed. A new photo had appeared in her gallery. It showed the abandoned house. Maya zoomed in. She could see herself standing outside the house.
But the photograph had been taken from inside the upstairs room.
And behind her stood the shadow. Maya slowly lowered the phone. Her bedroom clock read 11:59.
A whisper came from the hallway. “Maya…”
She stared at the locked bedroom door. The handle began to turn. Slowly. Then the clock struck midnight. The door opened. Beyond it was not her hallway. It was the abandoned house. And standing at the end of its dusty corridor was Maya's own reflection. It smiled. Then whispered: “You came back.” The bedroom door slammed shut.

The next morning, Maya was gone. Her family searched everywhere, but they found only one thing in her room—a photograph lying beside her phone. It showed the abandoned house. In the upstairs window stood Maya. And beside her stood the shadow. Below the photograph, someone had written: “One more room is ready.” That night, for the first time in twenty years, another light appeared in the abandoned house. Then came a whisper from the upstairs window. “Come closer…”
