Behind Door 27

Nora Vale

                                            Some Doors Are Better Left Unopened By Nora Vale (short story)

The hallway of Westbrook Hotel had twenty-six doors.
Everyone knew that.
But on Thursday evening, Daniel counted them again.
One. Two. Three…
Twenty-seven.
He stopped.

At the very end of the hallway stood a narrow black door with a silver number: 27
Daniel had stayed at the hotel many times, but he had never seen it before.
Curiosity won.
He turned the handle. The door opened into a small room filled with clocks. Hundreds of them. Wall clocks, pocket watches, tiny alarm clocks—every single one showed a different time.

Then Daniel noticed something even stranger. One clock was counting backward.
12:01.
12:00.
11:59.
A note was sitting beneath it.
“Whatever you do, do not let it reach midnight.”
Daniel checked his phone. 11:56 PM.
His stomach dropped. He grabbed the clock and tried to stop it. Nothing worked. 11:58.

Suddenly, the hotel lights flickered. 11:59.
Daniel heard footsteps outside the door.
Slow.
Heavy.
Getting closer.
The clock changed.
12:00.
Everything went silent. Daniel closed his eyes. Then— Click.
The lights came back on. The room was empty.
No clocks. No note. No mysterious door. Daniel was standing in the hotel hallway again. He laughed nervously and hurried toward the elevator. Then he noticed the brass directory beside the lift.
It listed every room in the hotel. Room 27 — Daniel Carter.
He stared at it. His phone buzzed. A message appeared from an unknown number: “Thank you for opening the door.” Daniel slowly looked back down the hallway. There were now twenty-eight doors.
