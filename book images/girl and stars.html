The Girl Who Collected Stars

Clara Wren

                                            A Sky Full of Secrets By Clara Wren

Every night, twelve-year-old Elia climbed onto the roof of her little house and watched the stars.
But Elia didn't just watch them. She collected them.
Whenever a star fell from the sky, it appeared as a tiny glowing stone on the ground.

Elia kept every one in a glass jar beside her bed. She had collected twenty-six.

Then, one night, something strange happened. A star fell directly into her hands.

It was colder than the others.
And it was whispering.
“Find the missing one.”

Elia stared at it.

The next morning, she counted her collection. Twenty-six.
She searched the sky.
One star was missing from the constellation above her village. That night, she followed a trail of tiny sparks into the forest.
The sparks led her to an old stone tower. Inside, she found a mysterious machine covered in gears and glass tubes.
At its center was a dark, empty space shaped exactly like a star.
A shadow moved behind her. “You shouldn't have come here.” Elia turned.
An old astronomer stood in the doorway. He explained that the machine had been built to protect the night sky. But one star had been stolen years ago, and without it, the constellations were slowly disappearing. Elia looked at the glowing star in her hand.

“Is this the missing one?” The astronomer nodded.
Elia placed it inside the machine. For a moment, nothing happened. Then the entire tower shook. WHOOM!
A beam of silver light shot into the sky. One by one, the missing stars returned. The constellations lit up brighter than ever.
The astronomer smiled. “You saved the sky.” Elia looked at her little jar.

The twenty-six stars inside had disappeared. She felt a little sad.
Then she noticed something glowing in her pocket.

A tiny new star. Bigger and brighter than all the others.

A note was wrapped around it: “For the girl who never stopped looking up.” Elia smiled. From that night on, she stopped collecting stars. She simply helped them find their way home.
